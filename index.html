Pushing electrons

Watch resonance happen one curved arrow at a time, then try drawing the arrows yourself.

What the arrows do

    Rules for pushing electrons
    • Atoms never move. Only electrons do: lone pairs and π bonds.
    • Single (σ) bonds stay put. They're the skeleton.
    • An arrow starts at electrons and points to where they end up, either a new bond or a new lone pair.
    • C, N and O can't go past eight electrons. If one arrow adds electrons to an atom, another arrow usually has to take some away.
    • Total charge never changes. It only moves.
    • No single structure is the real molecule. The real thing is the hybrid, a blend of all the contributors.
    • The best contributors have full octets, the fewest charges, and any negative charge on the more electronegative atom.
